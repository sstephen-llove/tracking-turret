# Project Rias — Development Journal

This journal documents my weekly progress building Rias, a robotic turret with computer vision, voice interaction, and AI-driven physical expressions. I also share updates on LinkedIn; this file keeps the build history alongside the code.

**Current stage:** Week 5, in progress.

## Week 1 — Research and Component Selection

I started by researching the electronics I would need and learning how the different components work. This included motors, motor drivers, servos, microcontrollers, power supplies, and the components needed for voice input and a small display.

I organized my research into a comprehensive shopping list in Google Sheets. This gave me a concrete starting point for the build and helped me connect the mechanical design to the electronics I planned to use.

**Milestone:** Completed the initial electronics research and component shopping list.

## Week 2 — CAD and Tolerances

I began designing the mechanical assembly in Onshape. My focus was on modeling the parts and working out the tolerances so that the printed components would fit together properly.

This stage meant thinking through how the parts would mount, rotate, and interact before I could test them physically. The CAD work laid the foundation for the support table and the moving assemblies that would follow.

**Milestone:** Started the mechanical design and established the intended fits between components.

## Week 3 — Makerspace Access and First Prints

I finally got access to the UGA MLC Makerspace and could start 3D printing my designs. By the end of the week, I had finished the table that would support the turret.

The finished table looked great, and the printed parts fit as intended. Seeing the tolerances work out in a physical assembly was a satisfying checkpoint after spending the previous week designing everything on screen.

**Milestone:** Completed the turret's support table and confirmed that its printed parts fit together correctly.

## Week 4 — Mechanical Assembly and Horizontal Tracking

I continued printing the remaining mechanical components, including the U-yoke, pinion gear, turntable, retaining clamp, and BB track. These parts brought the rotating assembly closer to a complete mechanism.

Alongside the printing, I worked on making Rias feel as snappy and responsive as possible. I got most of the electronics wired up and brought the horizontal, or X-axis, tracking to a reasonably good starting point.

This was the stage where the mechanical assembly, electronics, and tracking software began working together. There was still tuning to do, but the project was moving beyond individual parts into an integrated system.

**Milestone:** Wired most of the system and achieved initial horizontal tracking.

## Week 5 — Vertical Movement and Integration

**Status:** In progress.

With horizontal tracking working reasonably well, I shifted my attention to vertical, or Y-axis, movement. I designed a shaft that mounts to the servos on the U-yoke so the upper assembly can rotate up and down.

I worked on tuning the servo movement and control timing to improve responsiveness. My goal was to make the vertical movement feel as immediate as the horizontal movement. These improvements are still being evaluated through testing rather than measured performance benchmarks.

I also attached the camera and laser to the moving assembly. This is the current state of the build as I continue integrating and tuning the two axes.

**Milestone so far:** Designed the servo-mounted shaft, worked on vertical response, and mounted the camera and laser.

### Planned Next Stage

I plan to add a flywheel mechanism powered by two brushless drone motors to launch Nerf Rival rounds. This is a future addition and has not been completed.
